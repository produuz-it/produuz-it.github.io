Panel Sides and Coordinates ​

produuz.it uses one rule for the sides and the coordinates of a panel. The 3D viewer, the drawings, the lists and the machine files all start from it. This page explains the rule, how produuz.it reads it from your model, and which side each screen and each file looks from.

The rule in short ​

The rule

  • Every panel has a front and a back. The front is the outside face and holds the positive zones. The back is the inside face and holds the negative zones. Zone 0 is the frame.
  • X runs along the length of the panel, Y up its height, Z through its thickness, pointing towards the front.
  • The origin is the bottom-left corner of the frame, seen from the front, on the outer face of the frame.
  • All coordinates are in millimetres.

  1. Origin: the bottom-left corner of the frame seen from the front. It lies on the face of the frame that points to the outside, so the outside sheathing starts at Z = 0.
  2. X: along the panel, from left to right seen from the front.
  3. Y: up, from the bottom rail to the top rail.
  4. Z: through the thickness, from the inside to the outside.

Faces and zones ​

Every part of a panel has a zone: the layer it belongs to.

  1. Zone 0 is the frame: rails, studs and the other timbers of the structure.
  2. Z = 0 is the outer face of the frame. Positive zones have a positive Z, negative zones a negative Z.
  3. Negative zones are the layers of the inside face, counted outwards from the frame: -1 lies on the frame, -2 on top of -1, and so on.
  4. Positive zones are the layers of the outside face, counted the same way: 1 on the frame, 2 on top of 1.

Where the sides come from ​

produuz.it reads the outside of a panel from the zones your model gives to its parts: a positive zone is outside, a negative zone is inside.

  • IFC files: the zone is read with the Zone (layer) rule of your IFC Parsing Setup. A value that holds a B becomes a negative zone; anything else stays positive.
  • BTLx files: BTLx layer numbers have no sign. The Positive side zones and Negative side zones rules of the parsing setup say which layers belong to each face.

WARNING

If your model writes the outside layers with a B, or a BTLx setup has no side rules, produuz.it takes the inside for the outside. Every view and every file of that panel is then mirrored. Check one panel in the 3D viewer after setting up a new exporter: Front must show the outside face.

How produuz.it places a panel ​

When a panel is analysed, produuz.it works out its axes from the frame and the zones:

  1. X follows the longest top rail or bottom rail of zone 0.
  2. Y points from the bottom rail to the top rail.
  3. Z points from the negative zones to the positive zones. It is read from the plates of zones 1 to 3 and -1 to -3.

The origin is then the corner of the frame with the lowest X, the lowest Y and the highest Z.

For this to work, a panel needs:

  • at least one top rail or bottom rail in zone 0, as categorised by your parsing setup;
  • at least one plate in zones 1 to 3 or -1 to -3. A panel with plates on one face only works too.

When one of them is missing, the panel analysis says that the orientation could not be found. Fix the parsing setup and analyse the panel again.

Floors and roofs follow the same rule. The front is the face of the positive zones, whichever way the panel lies in the building, and Y points from the member you categorise as bottom rail to the one you categorise as top rail.

Best effort panel orientation

With the company setting Best effort panel orientation on, a panel whose orientation cannot be found from its rails and plates is placed from its geometry instead. The viewer and the drawings use it, but the front may come out on the wrong side. With the setting off, such a panel is shown in the coordinates of the model file. Machine files never use it: a panel without a proper orientation is left out of the file and listed in ERRORS.md. See Company settings.

The front and the back ​

Seen from the back, a panel is the front view mirrored. The same physical corner moves from one side of the picture to the other.

  1. Seen from the front, the origin is corner A, bottom-left, and X runs to the right.
  2. Drawings seen from the back keep measuring from corner A, which is now at the bottom-right. A length reads the same on the front and on the back drawing.
  3. MOB files with starting side inside measure the panel from the back, with their zero at corner B: the bottom-left corner seen from the back.

Drawings and MOB files on the inside face

A length on a drawing of a negative zone is measured from corner A. An X in a MOB file with starting side inside is measured from corner B, the other end of the panel. Both are correct; they start from opposite ends.

A plate has one reference and one drawing, whichever face it is mounted on. Two plates with the same shape in zone 1 and in zone -1 are the same plate in the lists. If they must be told apart, model them mirrored.

Production starting side ​

The starting side says which face of the panel is clad first on a panel line:

  • Inside (default): the frame is built, the inside face (negative zones) is clad, the panel is turned over, then the outside face (positive zones) is clad.
  • Outside: the other way round.

It also decides which face the MOB file measures the panel from. The value of the station that owns zone 0 applies to the whole line. See MOB v2.

Some machine settings change the side or the corner for one station only: Switch direction X-axis, Switch direction Y-axis and Machine zero on the Mobi One, and the face inversions on the Frame Station. Leave them at their defaults unless one station comes out mirrored. See Mobi One and Frame Station.

When a panel comes out wrong ​

  1. As modelled: seen from the front, the panel looks as it does from outside the building.
  2. Zone signs swapped: the inside layers have positive zones and the outside layers negative ones. The front shows the inside face, and the panel is mirrored left to right everywhere.
  3. Rails swapped: the top rail is categorised as bottom rail and the other way round. Y points down, so X turns too: the panel is turned half a turn.

Checklist for a new exporter ​

  1. The frame is zone 0.
  2. The outside layers are positive zones (F1, F2, or 1, 2) and the inside layers negative ones (B1, B2, or -1, -2), counted outwards from the frame.
  3. The top and bottom rails of the frame are categorised as such in the parsing setup.
  4. For BTLx, the parsing setup declares the positive and negative side zones.
  5. In the 3D viewer, Front shows the outside face, with the bottom rail at the bottom.
